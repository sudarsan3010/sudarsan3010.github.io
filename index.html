01 About

Following water from the rain to the aquifer — and on to the decisions that depend on it.

I'm a joint PhD candidate at the IITB-Monash Research Academy, a partnership between IIT Bombay and Monash University. My research is on groundwater sustainability in the Indian Ganga Basin, a global hotspot of groundwater depletion.

My work links the land surface to the aquifer, and the aquifer to decisions. I study how climate shapes groundwater recharge, what drives long-term depletion — pumping, climate variability and change, and river–aquifer exchange — and whether recharging more or pumping less does more to reverse it. I'm now turning that understanding into district-level crop choices that conserve groundwater while sustaining farm output.

Before my PhD I worked on groundwater salinity and isotope hydrology at the National Institute of Hydrology, Roorkee, and on satellite mapping of coastal ponds in the Indian Sundarbans for my Master's at ISRO's Regional Remote Sensing Centre (East).

02 Research

From rainfall to recommendations.

Four connected questions, each building on the one before. Together they move from understanding the groundwater system to helping manage it.

  1. Step 1 · Recharge

    How does climate shape recharge?

    Land-surface modelling with Noah-MP on HPC to build an ensemble recharge climatology for the basin, comparing subsurface schemes and the roles of soil moisture and evapotranspiration.

    • Noah-MP
    • HPC
    • Recharge

  2. Step 2 · Aquifer

    What drives depletion?

    A regional MODFLOW 6 groundwater model, calibrated with ensemble methods, that separates the effects of abstraction, climate, recharge and stream–aquifer exchange.

    • MODFLOW 6
    • PEST++ IES
    • Data assimilation

  3. Step 3 · Futures

    What happens next?

    CMIP6-driven projections under parameter, climate and abstraction uncertainty, testing whether supply-side (recharge) or demand-side (pumping) measures work better.

    • CMIP6
    • Uncertainty
    • Scenarios

  4. Step 4 · Decisions

    What should we grow?

    A machine-learning surrogate of the groundwater model, coupled with optimisation, to design district-level crop allocations that curb depletion and sustain farm output.

    • ML surrogate
    • Optimisation
    • Crop allocation

Study region

Why the Indian Ganga Basin?

It is a global hotspot of groundwater depletion, where farming relies heavily on pumped groundwater. That makes it the right place to ask which choices, from recharge to crop mix, can keep its aquifers productive.

Earlier work

2019–2020 · NIH Roorkee

M.Tech · ISRO RRSC-East
